\title{Does a Local SPD Metric Help Anisotropic Alpha-Complex Surface
Reconstruction? A Frozen Held-Out Evaluation and a Negative Result}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Scale selection for alpha-complex surface reconstruction is already well served
by density-adaptive (density-scaled) and normal-based anisotropic baselines. We
ask whether a local symmetric-positive-definite (SPD) metric derived from a
directed relation-tensor field adds value beyond these baselines, and answer it
with a predeclared, frozen held-out protocol on both a synthetic six-family panel
and a real-mesh corpus. The primary result is negative: on real held-out meshes
the prior-art normal-anisotropic baseline attains the best mean surface F-score
(0.868), and none of the local-metric variants exceeds it (the closest candidate
trails by $0.094$ in F-score, bootstrap 95\% CI $[-0.11,-0.08]$, clearing the
casewise baseline envelope in only 2 of 40 cases). Two secondary contributions
survive: a weighted (regular/power) alpha complex that changes connectivity
rather than rescoring a fixed triangulation strictly improves the density
baseline on both synthetic and real data, and a deployed fail-closed
exact-construction fallback provably never certifies an unvalidated result. We
also report that removal-based false-bridge interventions fail universally. We do
not claim to beat prior art.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The alpha complex is a classical tool for reconstructing a surface from a point
cloud, but a single global scale cannot simultaneously avoid over-fill and
topology loss when sampling density varies in space. Established remedies include
(i) \emph{density-adaptive} alpha, which normalizes each tetrahedron's
circumradius by the local $k$-nearest-neighbour (kNN) spacing (denoted B4), and
(ii) \emph{normal-anisotropic} alpha, which forms a regularized local
principal-component SPD metric with a planarity-weighted normal penalty (B5). A
recently proposed field construction yields a signed, trace-free relation tensor
from directed kNN scale contrast and receiver-direction imbalance, mapped through
bounded log-eigenvalues to a local SPD metric (P1), with a confidence-gated
conservative variant that falls back to the density baseline on low-confidence
cells (P2).

\paragraph{Question.} Does the added complexity of a local SPD metric provide
reconstruction value beyond the existing density (B4) and normal-anisotropic (B5)
baselines?

\paragraph{Contributions.} (1) A predeclared frozen held-out protocol with strict
information boundaries, applied to a synthetic panel and to real meshes. (2) A
quantitative, \emph{negative} answer: the local SPD metric does not beat the
normal-anisotropic baseline on real held-out data. (3) A weighted regular alpha
complex (M1) that changes connectivity and strictly improves the density baseline.
(4) A deployed fail-closed exact-construction fallback with a no-silent-false-safe
guarantee, and a comprehensive negative map of removal-based false-bridge
interventions.

\section{Related Work}
Our baselines instantiate standard ideas: alpha shapes and their weighted
(regular/power) generalization; density-adaptive and normal-based anisotropic
reconstruction; and persistence- and resampling-stability-based automatic scale
selection. Watertight reconstruction via inside/outside labelling of Delaunay
tetrahedra is a distinct family we discuss but do not implement. Rather than
proposing a new reconstructor, we stress-test the \emph{added value} of a local
SPD metric with a rigorous frozen held-out comparison.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Baselines and candidates}
B0--B3 are a convex hull, a fixed normalized-radius alpha, an exhaustive
critical-scale oracle (allowed to use the dense reference), and an unlabeled
persistence-plus-resampling-stability selector. B4 divides each tetrahedron's
Euclidean circumradius by the geometric-mean kNN spacing of its vertices. B5
combines a regularized local PCA SPD metric with the density normalization and a
planarity-weighted normal penalty. P1 forms the relation-tensor SPD metric; P2
adds a $\max(\mathrm{P1},\mathrm{B4})$ guard on low-confidence cells. Critically,
B4, B5, P1 and P2 all select subcomplexes of \emph{one fixed isotropic Delaunay
triangulation} by a per-cell scalar threshold, so a false-bridge tetrahedron
already present in that triangulation cannot be removed by rescoring without
collateral damage.

\subsection{M1: weighted regular alpha complex}
M1 changes the connectivity itself. Each point receives a density weight
$w_i=(s\cdot \mathrm{spacing}_i)^2$, and the regular (weighted) triangulation is
obtained as the lower convex hull of the lift
$p_i\mapsto(p_i,\lVert p_i\rVert^2-w_i)$; with $w=0$ this reproduces the ordinary
Delaunay triangulation, so M1 strictly generalizes B4. Cells are scored by the
\emph{proper} weighted (power) circumradius normalized by kNN spacing. Scoring the
weighted connectivity with the ordinary circumradius introduces nonmanifold and
Betti regressions; the power circumradius removes them. Large weights submerge
points from the triangulation, so the weight scale is capped.

\subsection{G4: deployed fail-closed exact fallback}
A built-in exact Euclidean Delaunay backend is run under host validation and, on
acceptance, its validated connectivity is injected into the actual selection path.
On any refusal---a point-count cap, exact cospherical ambiguity, host rejection,
or backend timeout/malformed output---the method fails closed to a floating
construction that is explicitly labelled non-exact, recording the specific reason.
The certified outcome is reachable only after full validation; every failure mode
records a reason and is never silently treated as safe. This certifies only the
base Delaunay connectivity, not the anisotropic complex.

\subsection{Frozen held-out protocol}
Every method's scale (and M1's weight scale) is frozen on a \emph{calibration}
set; no tuning is permitted on the held-out set. On the synthetic panel we freeze
on a six-family calibration set and evaluate four held-out density/noise/geometry
profiles over three paired seeds, testing each candidate against the strict
casewise per-endpoint baseline envelope. On real data we freeze on a calibration
mesh set and evaluate a disjoint held-out mesh set across density and noise
splits. References and labels enter calibration and evaluation only, never
held-out selection.

\section{Experiments and Results}
\subsection{Synthetic preflight}
The frozen confidence-fallback variant P2 preserves the density guard (zero
violations) but fails the strict casewise envelope on all four held-out profiles
(mean F-score margins $-0.048$, $+0.0002$, $-0.049$, $-0.049$): a small mean
F-score gain is not evidence of value. On calibration, M1 at weight scale
$s=0.375$ strictly dominates B4 on every endpoint (Table~\ref{tab:syn}), whereas
the normal-anisotropic baseline is topologically degenerate on this panel (Betti
error 105, 175 nonmanifold edges). On the held-out envelope M1 is 4--5$\times$
closer than the previous best (per-profile F-margins
$-0.0105/{-}0.0005/{-}0.0003/{-}0.0109$) but does not clear it; the residual gap
is dominated by one family's false bridges.

\subsection{Real held-out data}
Table~\ref{tab:real} is the central result. On real meshes the normal-anisotropic
baseline B5 is clearly the strongest (mean F-score 0.868), and no local-metric
candidate exceeds it; the closest, M1, trails by $0.094$ with a bootstrap 95\% CI
that excludes zero. This reverses the synthetic picture: on smooth real meshes the
well-defined normals make B5 excel, while its synthetic degeneracy was an artifact
of a panel pathology.

\subsection{A negative map of false-bridge interventions}
Attempts to remove false bridges by deletion fail universally. Per-cell penalties,
layered boundary-owner peeling, connected risk-region removal, a resampling
persistence gate (discrimination AUC $\approx 0.50$), global multiplier backoff,
reference-free fail-closed routing, and oriented-normal removal/offset/signed
labelling all regress geometry or topology. Two causes: removing a cell from a
fixed complex exposes its interior faces as new boundary; and in the hardest
family the inter-surface gap is smaller than the sample spacing (41\% of kNN edges
cross the two ground-truth sheets), so no \emph{local} method can separate them.
Only the connectivity change (M1) helps, and it does not resolve that
under-resolved case.

\section{Discussion}
The real held-out evaluation shows quantitatively that the local SPD metric
(P1/P2) provides no value beyond the established normal-anisotropic baseline. The
synthetic panel misleads by unfairly weakening that baseline through an
under-resolved pathology. What remains useful is that changing connectivity (M1)
improves the density baseline, and that an exact-construction fallback can be
deployed safely on the actual selection path.

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
The evaluation uses a local corpus, not a licensed leaderboard, and topology
endpoints on complex shells are noisy. The one untested path is a global graph-cut
inside/outside tetrahedron labelling, which requires a separate large
implementation. We conclude that a PFTF-derived local SPD metric does not surpass
prior-art anisotropic alpha on real held-out data. The improved density baseline
(M1) and the fail-closed exact fallback are reusable contributions, and the
universal failure of removal-based bridge interventions is useful negative
evidence for future work.
